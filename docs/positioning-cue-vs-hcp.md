# Positioning: why CUE wins the unscheduled-maintenance fight against Housecall Pro

## The evidence

A Housecall Power Pros Facebook post (Blaine MacKenzie, Oct 2026, 4.2K reactions, 634 comments, 788 shares) asks a simple question about HCP's unscheduled service-plan jobs:

> **"The problem is that HCP generates the service plan jobs and drops them into Unscheduled, and that's where it ends. There's no way we've found to:**
> - **Put the unscheduled jobs on a map**
> - **Pull a report of just this month's unscheduled maintenance**
> - **Group them by town/area so we can route them"**
>
> **"With 500 jobs a month, scrolling a list doesn't cut it. When a tech frees up at 1pm, we need to quickly see what's open near him."**

Then the tell — the workaround list says it all: *"export to Excel or Google Maps? ... use tags, a dummy tech, or a separate calendar? ... a third-party add-on or Zapier? ... or did you switch to something else for this part?"*

Nothing in the post or its answers names a feature in HCP that fixes it. HCP's own docs show why: unscheduled jobs live in a separate **drawer**, while map views cover only *scheduled* calendar work; ZIP-code "areas" exist but route-based reporting is still documented as **"coming soon."** So the backlog has to be scheduled or exported before it can be visualized. That's the wedge.

## What to say

**Headline:** "CUE turns your unscheduled maintenance backlog into routes the same morning a tech opens his truck."

**Three-line pitch:**

> A Housecall Pro user with 500 monthly maintenance jobs posted recently: *"HCP generates the service plan jobs and drops them into Unscheduled, and that's where it ends."* No map. No monthly report. No town/area grouping.
>
> CUE gives you the board HCP doesn't: your unscheduled open jobs, **grouped by real town/area, one click turns an area into a Google Maps route**, and a month-by-month report of what's piling up — with no dummy tech tags, no Excel exports, no Zapier.
>
> And because CUE watches the same data, it also flags the slowdown you probably missed in the same building: which jobs waited how long before a tech ever got assigned, and which service-plan customers haven't been visited at all.

## Why this is a real differentiation, not a tick-box

- Incumbents optimize for *scheduling what you already scheduled*. HCP's map/route tools assume you first commit a job to a tech and a time slot. CUE's board is the first screen that shows the backlog *before* someone commits it — group by area, decide by distance, drag the day into a real calendar after.
- The pain is operational, not cosmetic: 500 unscheduled jobs in a drawer means the office *still* routes by phone and paper. Any small operator reading that thread sees themselves.
- It's demo-able in 30 seconds with a realistic seed: three towns, five jobs, one tech free at 1pm. Click Riverside → Maps route opens. Done. No integrations to set up first, unlike most "platform switching" conversations.

## Honest counters the HCP rep will raise

| Their move | Our answer |
|---|---|
| "You can map it once it's scheduled." | Right — but *scheduling it* is the step users need help with. The Facebook thread is literally people asking how to see work before it's scheduled. |
| "You can use ZIP-code 'areas'." | For coloring the calendar, yes. Route-based reporting is still "coming soon" per their own docs. CUE ships it, with grouping, report, and export. |
| "Just use our Jobs-list CSV." | That's the workaround this post's comment section already does in Excel — accepting it validates the gap. |

## Do-not-overclaim list

1. Don't claim CUE drives the truck — no live GPS/telemetry. Say *"route planning around your real addresses."*
2. Don't claim one-click migration from HCP. Say *two-way CSV import is live, native sync is on the roadmap after the pilot.*
3. Don't claim CUE replaces HCP for basic scheduling — **it layers on top for the leak-and-recovery work** and wins deals where the backlog is the pain.

## Where the pitch goes in the sales motion

- **Discovery question:** *"How many open maintenance jobs are sitting in Unscheduled right now — and how does anyone see them by town?"* Let the prospect describe the workaround aloud.
- **Demo:** the unscheduled board with their job counts, grouped by their own towns. Not a slide — a live click of Route → Maps.
- **Close:** the *$299/mo Operator plan* framed against the -hours of office time the board replaces (the 1pm-tech scenario), with the reminder cadence and agreements leak as second-mover features in the same demo.
